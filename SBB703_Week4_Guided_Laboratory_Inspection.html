What is an inspection?

An inspection checks whether a laboratory meets required standards and follows its written processes. The goal is to make sure laboratory practices put patient safety first. Inspectors observe the work, speak with laboratory staff, and review records. AABB calls its inspectors assessors.

Your role today

Whether you are new to inspections or have participated before, this activity guides you through one case. You are a clinical laboratory scientist joining Morgan, the lead assessor, at Cedar Bay Hospital to review one completed, nonurgent red blood cell (RBC) transfusion.

The visit begins: an opening meeting

Before the laboratory review begins, the inspection team meets with hospital and laboratory leaders. This is the opening meeting. Everyone introduces themselves, confirms which services will be reviewed, and agrees on the plan for the visit.

Who is in the room?

The inspection team: Trained reviewers with expertise in the services being assessed. AABB assessors volunteer their time. Members may have different specialties, such as donor services, transfusion testing, or cellular therapy. The mix depends on the services under review.

The hospital team: Leaders responsible for those services. At this meeting, you meet the transfusion service medical director, laboratory manager, and Avery, the quality coordinator. Avery helps manage the laboratory’s policies and quality records.

Morgan explains which areas the assessors will review and who can help them find records. You will focus on RBC selection. Before leaving the meeting, Morgan reminds you to respect patient privacy and let laboratory staff finish patient-care tasks.

How to complete the activity

  1. Read from top to bottom. Each step explains what you are doing and why.
  2. Write a concise but understandable answer in each box. One complete sentence is usually enough. State your point and the reason; you do not need a paragraph.
  3. Use only the information shown so far. Choose Continue to reveal the next step. Earlier sections stay above.
  4. Save your conclusion. Read the hospital’s response and add one brief comment. Then download your .txt file and upload it to Canvas.

Your first impression does not need to be correct. Your instructor reviews how you use the evidence. You may revise your notes. Your first saved responses are kept.

Use only the information provided. Do not enter real patient information. Nothing is sent to Canvas automatically.

Sources used to prepare this activity

No extra reading is required. The Standards are summarized in plain language. This walkthrough focuses on one record review, not a complete inspection.

AABB. Standards for Blood Banks and Transfusion Services. 35th edition. Standards 5.14.9 and 5.15.3 (RBC selection), 7.1 and 7.2.4 (event review and patient safety), and 9.1 (corrective action); course BB/TS assessment tool, pages 63–64, 82–84, and 90–91. Effective April 1, 2026.

AABB. Assessor Information, About AABB Accreditation, and Accreditation Process. Assessor roles, expertise, and the assessment approach.

AABB. Assessor Continuing Education Program. October 12, 2018. “International Assessments.” Background on meetings, tours, and interviewing, not current administrative rules.

Canadian Blood Services. Pre-transfusion testing. Australian Red Cross Lifeblood. Compatibility testing and Component compatibility. Clinical background.

AABB. Quality Toolkit. System-focused investigation, corrective action, and effectiveness review.

Australian Red Cross Lifeblood. Delayed haemolytic transfusion reactions. Background on antibodies becoming undetectable and the importance of antibody history.

Record E supplies additional case evidence. The workflow changes and 20-case review are teaching examples, not prescribed AABB methods or a required audit sample size. The hospital’s response applies to routine issue; emergency-release decisions remain outside this case.
