CASE A · A-027

Step 3

Determine whether antibody is coating the patient’s red cells and whether the plasma reacts with those cells.

These tests use different materials. Read the description of each before interpreting its result.

DAT

The direct antiglobulin test examines the patient’s washed red cells for IgG or complement already attached in the body. Patient plasma is not added for the DAT.

Autocontrol

The laboratory combines the patient’s plasma with the patient’s red cells and tests that mixture. It asks whether those materials react under the test conditions.

Eluate

The laboratory releases antibody from the patient’s coated red cells into a new liquid, the eluate. That liquid is tested against reagent panel cells to investigate the antibody that was attached.

The final-wash control is the liquid from the last cell wash before elution. Its negative result helps show that the eluate reactions are not explained by antibody left in unwashed plasma.

The IgG-positive DAT, positive autocontrol, and broadly reactive eluate support a warm-autoantibody interpretation in this case. Recent transfusion still requires consideration of an alloimmune process. These serologic results do not, by themselves, establish hemolysis.

The practical problem remains: the original plasma reacts with every panel cell. An additional alloantibody could be hidden within those reactions. The next step is to choose how to remove the interfering antibody while preserving the ability to investigate what remains.

CASE A · A-027

Step 4

Use the transfusion history to choose the source of cells for adsorption.

Adsorption means incubating plasma with red cells so that antibodies bind to those cells. After centrifugation, the laboratory removes the liquid above the cells and tests that recovered liquid.

The cells can remove an antibody only when they provide a suitable target for it. They do not distinguish an autoantibody from an alloantibody. This is why the cell source and antigen profile are important.

ApproachCells usedWhat this means for Case A
Autologous adsorptionThe patient’s own red cellsNot selected. Transfusion 21 days ago means the sample may also contain transfused donor cells. Those cells could remove an alloantibody from the plasma.
Allogeneic adsorptionSelected donor red cells with known antigen profilesSelected. Different donor profiles allow the IRL to track which antibodies could remain in each separate plasma aliquot.
IRL decision for this patient

Because the patient was transfused within the preceding three months, use differential allogeneic adsorption. “Differential” means that the selected donor cells have deliberately different antigen profiles. For this workup, the IRL uses a PEG adsorption method.

CASE A · A-027

Step 6

Prepare three separate adsorption tubes and incubate each with its assigned donor cells.

Keep each plasma aliquot separate from start to finish. Do not pass the same plasma through A, then B, then C.

The letters on each enlarged red cell identify the R-H antigens it expresses. E and e are different antigens, as are C and c. Follow the purple anti-E: it can bind only where an E antigen is present.

  1. Wash and pack the donor cells.

    Wash each sample according to the IRL procedure, centrifuge, and remove the saline above the packed cells. Residual saline adds unwanted dilution and can make a weak antibody harder to detect.

  2. Label the tubes before adding plasma.

    A receives R₁R₁ cells, B receives R₂R₂ cells, and C receives rr cells. Record the donor-cell lot associated with each tube.

  3. Add a separate plasma aliquot to each tube.

    Retain some original plasma for comparison. For planning, three 1 mL aliquots would require 3 mL of plasma before other testing, transfer losses, and repeat work. Use the specimen and volume requirements in the IRL procedure.

  4. Add the assigned packed cells and PEG.

    Use the ratios in the validated PEG method. PEG is the enhancement medium in this adsorption; it is not a treatment that removes antigens from the cells.

  5. Incubate at 37°C.

    The warm-reactive antibody binds to the adsorbing cells during incubation. An alloantibody can also bind if those cells express its antigen.

Published PEG method example

A published serum-based method uses equal volumes of serum, packed red cells, and PEG, with a 15-minute incubation at 37°C. Plasma use and the later test of the recovered PEG-containing liquid require a validated method for that specimen type. The method used by one IRL is not automatically interchangeable with another.

You have prepared the mixtures. You have not yet identified the antibodies in the recovered plasma. That happens after the cells and liquid are separated.

CASE A · A-027

Step 7

Separate the liquid from the cells and determine whether another adsorption round is needed.

After incubation, centrifuge the tube. The red cells form a pellet at the bottom. The liquid above them is the supernatant. Transfer the supernatant into a new tube without carrying over the pellet. For the rest of this case, that recovered liquid is called adsorbed plasma.

Before interpreting a panel, the IRL checks whether interfering reactivity has been adequately removed. This is the adsorption endpoint. Here, each recovered aliquot is tested with fresh cells of its corresponding adsorbing profile, together with the procedure’s selected reagent-cell checks.

Read the first row: A still reacts 1+ with fresh A-profile cells after the first round. The IRL therefore incubates the recovered A plasma with a fresh portion of the same donor-cell profile. After the second round, that check is 0. The same sequence occurs in B and C.

A second round means transferring the recovered plasma onto fresh cells of the same assigned profile. A stays with R₁R₁, B stays with R₂R₂, and C stays with rr. Record the rounds and recovered volume. Additional PEG is handled according to the procedure, not automatically added again.

A negative endpoint check does not mean the plasma contains no alloantibody. For example, A cells lack E, so anti-E can remain in A while its corresponding-cell check is negative. You must now test the recovered plasma against cells with a wider antigen profile.

IRL REPORT

Final report / A-027

Interpretation: Serologic reactivity is consistent with a warm-reactive autoantibody with underlying anti-E. The DAT is IgG-positive and C3d-negative; the acid eluate is broadly reactive. Differential PEG allogeneic adsorption with selected R1R1, R2R2, and rr cells resolved the broad plasma reactivity and demonstrated anti-E. No additional clinically significant alloantibodies were detected within the antigen coverage and sensitivity of the completed investigation.

Transfusion guidance: Honor anti-E and all historical clinically significant antibodies. Select ABO-compatible, E-negative RBCs and perform the institution's validated serologic compatibility procedure. Use an appropriately adsorbed aliquot that preserves anti-E detection when that approach is authorized. Additional phenotype/genotype matching may be indicated.

Limitations: Adsorption can weaken or remove antibodies. Allogeneic adsorption does not reliably exclude antibodies to antigens present on all adsorbing cells, including some high-prevalence antigens. Warm autoantibody interference may cause incompatibility with unadsorbed plasma. The serologic findings do not establish hemolysis; correlate with clinical and hemolysis findings.

For anti-E, the laboratory also considers the patient’s c status and its antigen-matching policy. This patient has a historical c-positive molecular result. Do not select units by the weakest incompatible reaction. Urgent transfusion decisions are made with the transfusion medicine physician rather than delayed solely to finish adsorption.
